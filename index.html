Jun 2025 – present · Deposco

Building the unified search growth engine

Challenge

A mid-market B2B SaaS company with three search channels run as three separate line items, each reporting its own vanity metrics and none of them tied to revenue in a way finance trusted.

Strategy

Treat organic, paid and AI search as one program with one scorecard: pipeline created and bookings closed, pulled from the CRM and reconciled with finance. Rebuild each channel around what the ICP actually buys, then fund them through a single annual plan.

What I owned

Strategy and measurement across all three channels; organic and AI search hands-on; paid search strategy and account design with an agency partner on execution; the attribution model, the board reporting and the US and UK annual operating plan models.

Decisions

Retired Performance Max and value-based bidding where B2B signal was too thin. Consolidated overlapping content into single pages per buying intent. Stood up a dedicated AI Search source in HubSpot rather than letting assistant-driven demos fall into “direct.”

Result

Search-sourced pipeline up 75% year over year and closed-won ACV up 36%; search is now the company’s largest measured pipeline source. Both AOP models adopted by the CMO.

2025 – 2026 · Deposco · US Search Awards Silver

Turning AI search into a measurable revenue channel

Challenge

Buyers were starting in ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews, but nothing in the stack could see it. No visibility baseline, no attribution, and a leadership team rightly skeptical of any channel that could not be measured.

Strategy

Measure before optimizing. Track visibility prompt by prompt against a competitive set, build the CRM plumbing so every assistant-driven demo is attributed, then earn citations where the assistants actually look: consolidated bottom-of-funnel pages and third-party roundups.

What I owned

The whole channel: the 116-prompt tracking program in Profound, the HubSpot source and attribution model, the content consolidation, the placements, and the executive reporting that made it a budget line.

Decisions

Set a 20% visibility goal and reported against it monthly. Chose commercial-investigation prompts over informational ones. Published the methodology and its limitations to the C-suite rather than only the wins.

Result

46.8% LLM visibility against the 20% goal and #1 on the priority topic. 15 demos became 10 pipeline opportunities and 2 closed-won deals in year one, a 67% demo-to-opportunity rate against roughly 20% for the website overall. Silver, Best AI Search Optimization Campaign, US Search Awards 2026.

2022 – 2025 · Manhattan Associates · US Search Awards Finalist

Running enterprise search across 13 regions through the AI Overviews shift

Challenge

A NASDAQ-listed supply chain software leader with 13 regional markets and 14 language sites, a Drupal-to-Optimizely replatform on the calendar, and AI Overviews about to cut top-result click-through by a third. The program had to grow while the ground moved underneath it.

Strategy

Report search in the currency finance used, sales-accepted-opportunity ARR, and run every region against the same scorecard. Protect rankings through the replatform first, then go after page-one coverage and SERP features, including the new AI Overview placements, for the product terms that fed pipeline.

What I owned

Global SEO and paid search strategy; product ownership of GA4; the replatform SEO plan and sign-off across all 14 language sites; the dashboards, executive QBRs and regional training that ran the program; the US Search Awards entry.

Decisions

Treated the replatform as a migration with zero acceptable ranking loss rather than a redesign. Prioritized SERP features and AI Overview placements for primary product terms ahead of raw ranking counts. Built region-level training so local teams could execute without a central bottleneck.

Result

Organic traffic up 21% and organic-sourced SAO ARR up 17% in 2024, with traffic still up 15% in 1H 2025 as AI Overviews took a third of top-result clicks. Ranking queries up 62%; page-one rankings plus SERP features up 255%, including AI Overview placements for primary product terms. Replatform completed with rankings preserved. Paid search beat its SAO ARR target by 52% at a consistent 10× ROAS. Finalist, Best Use of Search – B2B (SEO), US Search Awards 2025.

2016 – 2022 · Acadia · Director of SEO

Scaling an agency SEO practice by 233%

Challenge

A small SEO team inside a growing digital agency, with inconsistent delivery across hundreds of clients and no system for turning junior hires into reliable practitioners.

Strategy

Run SEO as a business line, not a service desk. Own the P&L, build the delivery system and training program first, then hire into it, and tie the practice’s growth to client outcomes that sales could sell.

What I owned

The practice and its P&L: hiring, four managers and their teams, playbooks and training, pricing, and new-business pitches alongside sales. Reported to the COO, accountable to the C-suite.

Decisions

Standardized delivery into repeatable playbooks before scaling headcount. Blended in-house, offshore and freelance capacity to protect margin. Kept client delivery intact through a merger and acquisition.

Result

A 20-person practice, SEO revenue up 233% as the top-performing team company-wide, and clients growing keywords, traffic and leads 27% a year on average. The agency won Ad Age Small Agency of the Year Silver in 2022.

A little about how I got here

Computer
scientist
by training.

I came into search through code. A computer science degree, a junior developer job, and a Shopify store I ran on the side taught me that search is a systems problem before it is a marketing one: architecture, signal, measurement.

At Acadia that instinct turned into a business. I started as a technical analyst and ended up running the SEO practice and its P&L, twenty people through four managers, hundreds of clients, and the playbooks that let the team grow faster than the agency around it.

Manhattan Associates taught me scale and governance: thirteen regions, fourteen language sites, a replatform, and the discipline of reporting search to an enterprise finance team.

In 2025 I left that comfortable seat for a mid-market challenger at the moment many peers were leaving search altogether. The bet was that buyers were moving, not disappearing, and that the first team to measure AI search like any other channel would own it. The results on this page are what that bet produced. That is the work now: running search as a growth engine, measuring AI search like any other channel, and writing up what we learn so other teams can use it.

Approach and philosophy

How I run
search.

Six principles behind the results above.

Strategy
Priorities, not volume

The plan starts from business priorities and what the ICP actually needs to buy, then works back to the keywords, prompts and pages. Search volume and assumptions never set the agenda.

Pipeline and bookings
Reported in dollars

Pipeline created and bookings closed, pulled from the CRM, reconciled with finance and presented to the board. Traffic and rankings are inputs, not the scorecard.

AI search / AEO / GEO
A channel, not a test

A dedicated AI Search source in the CRM plus prompt-level visibility tracking, so assistant-driven pipeline and closed deals are measured like any other channel.

Organic resilience
Built to last

Architecture and content built for search engines and retrieval systems (RAG, LLMs) at the same time, so optimizing for AI search strengthens organic instead of cannibalizing it.

Paid search
Built for B2B, not the algorithm

Long sales cycles starve automated bidding of signal, so the account is built by hand: tight match types, product tiers by pipeline velocity, and branded capture isolated from non-branded demand so the lift can be proven.

CRO and testing
Tests, not opinions

Hypothesis, control, window, limitations. Landing pages, bids and content changes earn their place through experiments, and negative results get written up too.
